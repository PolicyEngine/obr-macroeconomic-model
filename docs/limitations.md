# Limits: read this before quoting a number

This page is the full list of what the model can and cannot do, channel by channel, with the measured numbers and their history. It moved here from the README, unchanged except where noted in the changelog.

The emulator is honest about its own limits in code (`df.attrs` carries a
warning on every result that has one), but the limits are severe and easy to
miss. In one place:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Anchored fit to the October 2026 EFO** | GDP 0.24% MAPE, consumption 0.40% (2025Q1–2027Q4; 0.30% and 0.49% against the March 2026 EFO). **By construction** — the add-factors are computed as `EFO − model` and added back. It proves the anchoring machinery works, nothing about forecast skill. And only consumption is re-derived: every other term in the GDP identity is held at its EFO value, so the GDP number is the consumption number diluted by the consumption share. **Unemployment reproduces the EFO, also by construction**: employment and the participation rate are EFO inputs, and the forecast 16+ population is the path they imply through l.175 (`POP16 = 100·(ULFSU + ETLFS)/PART16`, equal to the ONS series over history), so the labour-force identity l.163 returns the EFO's rate (5.07% at 2027Q4, 4.17% at 2031Q1). It is not an unemployment forecast: nothing in the model moves unemployment in response to a shock, because total employment is set from hours inputs (`ETLFS = 1000·HWA/AVH`, l.153). The opt-in `run_reform(..., employment_response=True)` passes the listing's market sector labour demand (l.145) through to total employment; it gives about −15,000 jobs after 12 quarters for a 1p basic-rate rise and close to nothing for government consumption, with the wrong sign in some quarters; that run carries a warning ([demand_overlays.md](demand_overlays.md)). The anchored GDP gap grows with the horizon, to 0.77% by 2031Q1 (`results/site_figures.json`). Before 18 Nov 2026 the population was held flat and the anchored rate drained to 1.05% by 2031Q1; those figures are in [forecasting_framework.md](forecasting_framework.md) and [calibration_scorecard.md](calibration_scorecard.md). |
| **Reform baseline** | `run_reform` scores every reform around this anchored baseline: the add-factors are frozen into the baseline *and* the shocked run, so they set the level and cancel in the delta. Its own baseline solve tracks the EFO to GDP 0.26%, consumption 0.42%, household income 0.00% under the default nominal-GDP pin (October 2026 EFO; 0.31% and 0.51% on the March 2026 EFO) (0.03% under the published closure, the previous default) (household income and, under the investment closure, total investment are held on the EFO by a level add-factor rather than by deleting their identities, so reforms can still move them). Before 2026-10 the reform baseline silently ran free — 4.35% off on GDP. |
| **Free-running (add-factors off)** | GDP 4.24%, consumption 7.23%, business investment 7.34%, household income 3.10%, company profits 12.88%, current account 0.91% of GDP, unemployment 0.62pp (October 2026 EFO, 18 Nov 2026, with the forecast 16+ population on the EFO-implied path; held flat, it had flattered GDP and consumption, which read 4.05% and 6.92%), under the default nominal-GDP pin (the OBR's commented-out l.690, `GDPMPS = PGDP*GDPM/100`; see [nominal_gdp_pin.md](nominal_gdp_pin.md)). Under the published closure, the previous default (still available as `nominal_gdp_pin=False`), the same solve gives GDP 4.28%, consumption 7.30%, business investment 14.19%, household income 4.04%, company profits 22.47% and current account 1.33% of GDP (before `TYWHH` was closed: 4.51%, 7.67%, 18.21%, 6.51%, 109.96% and 3.32%; on the March 2026 EFO: 4.14%, 6.92%, 17.19%, 5.97%, 43.64% and 2.11%; before the receipts identities went live, #56: 4.32%, 7.22%, 15.28%, 6.18%, 82.77% and 3.02%); its company-profits and current-account figures measure solver drift, not fit ([#42](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/42)): nominal GDP and the investment deflator `PIF` are one identity written twice (l.337/l.688), so `PIF` random-walks under the de-seeded solve and profits, a residual of nominal GDP, swing with it. 11 of 21 headline variables are computed at all; the other 10 are held at the OBR's value. This gap is a property of **this emulator**, not an OBR forecast error: in the OBR's own model GDP is an input (the expenditure identity solves for inventories), so their model cannot miss it. What the gap measures is this repo's closure swap (GDP made endogenous) plus the OBR inputs and constants that are not published and had to be pulled from the ONS or seeded. The ended EU budget payments `GNP4` and `EUVAT` are held at zero from 2021 ([#62](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/62)); with them regenerated, the default-closure current account read 2.19% of GDP and company profits 17.90%. See [calibration_scorecard.md](calibration_scorecard.md). |
| **Government-consumption multiplier** | 1.01 on impact, drifting up to 1.04 by quarter 12 under the default nominal-GDP pin (1.00 and flat under the published closure, the previous default), because the shock lands directly in the expenditure identity. The only second round is that the extra nominal GDP lands in company profits (wages are exogenous) and a small part reaches household income and consumption (+£16m to +£50m on a £1.25bn/quarter shock). The OBR's published impact multiplier for **current** spending is **0.6**, decaying to zero. This model overstates it by ~69% on impact and by more thereafter. It is accounting, not a multiplier. The model cannot produce the OBR's multiplier; `run_reform(..., published_conventions=True)` (off by default) imposes the published 0.6-fading convention as a labelled add-factor path — judgement re-applied, not a model result. **Borrowing rises by the cash cost** (since 18 Nov 2026): a default `CGG` shock also moves the imposed nominal totals `CGGPS`, `CGGPSPSF` and `PSCE` by shock × baseline deflator, so `CGNB` (l.524) and public sector net borrowing rise by about £1.34bn to £1.42bn a quarter for £1.25bn of real spending. That is accounting: receipts have no equation and do not respond. `nominal_consistent=False` gives the earlier real-only shock, which leaves borrowing at exactly zero, lowers the government deflator and makes measured business investment read +16% to +24% of the shock ([nominal_gdp_pin.md](nominal_gdp_pin.md)). **Imports do not leak** (21 Nov 2026): total imports `M` have no equation, the services equation (l.223) is dropped for missing inputs, and non-oil goods imports are the residual `MNOG = M − MS − MOIL` (l.217), so for a shock of 1% of GDP they fall by about £190m to £250m a quarter as oil imports rise. The opt-in `run_reform(..., import_response=True)` lets services follow l.223 and goods follow import-weighted demand (the listing's l.211 weights, unit elasticity assumed, oil counted once): imports then rise by 9% to 12% of the shock and GDP per pound falls from 1.01–1.04 to 0.90–0.93 (0.97–1.00 if only the listing's services equation responds). It is a labelled judgement overlay, and still far from 0.6 ([demand_overlays.md](demand_overlays.md)). |
| **Welfare and other current spending** | Social benefits (`CGSB`) and other current grants (`CGOTR`) reach household income and consumption, the same channel as a household tax change. For +£1,000m a quarter of `CGSB`, household income rises by £1,001m / £1,013m / £1,028m and GDP by £92m / £191m / £323m at q1 / q4 / q12: current-price GDP per pound of 0.10 / 0.21 / 0.37, against the OBR's welfare multiplier of **0.6**, fading. The model is a sixth of the OBR's figure on impact and builds where the OBR's fades, because the published consumption equation adjusts gradually and has no separate response for benefit recipients. **Borrowing rises by the shock** (since 25 Nov 2026): the default shock also moves public sector current expenditure `PSCE`, which the listing imposes as a total. Before that, and still with `nominal_consistent=False`, the listing's subsidy residual (`CGSUBP`, l.504) absorbed the shock: borrowing moved by exactly zero, subsidies fell by £1,000m, taxes less subsidies on products rose by £1,000m, profits fell by £917m / £820m / £678m, household income rose by only £867m / £887m / £914m and GDP by £80m / £167m / £286m ([#88](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/88)). **Every other line of `PSCE` still has this problem** (local authority benefits `LASBHH`, grants to local authorities `CGNCGA`, pay, procurement, debt interest shocked directly and the rest of l.504): `run_reform` sets `df.attrs["subsidy_residual_warning"]` and warns. For `LASBHH` the result is wrong-signed: central government net borrowing *falls* by the shock. Figures from one local solve on 25 Nov 2026, pinned by `tests/test_solver.py::test_welfare_shock_raises_borrowing_and_household_income_one_for_one`. |
| **Government-investment multiplier** | **No channel under the default closure.** `IF` has no live equation in the published model, so `CGIPS → GGIPS → GGI → IF` never reaches GDP: `delta_IF` is exactly zero and the GDP residue is wrong-signed deflator noise. `run_reform` warns and names two labelled routes. `investment_closure=True` switches on the listing's commented-out `IF` identity (l.86), and +£3bn a quarter then moves GDP by +£3.08bn / +£2.90bn / +£2.90bn at q1 / q4 / q12 (`results/demo_shocks.json`): identity passthrough at about 1.0 per real pound, with no taper and no crowding-out, labelled `mechanical_passthrough` (the nominal-GDP pin is off under that closure). `published_conventions=True` instead imposes the OBR's 1.0-fading convention as a labelled add-factor path. The OBR's capital multiplier is 1.0 on impact, tapering. |
| **VAT and fuel duty** | By default a VAT rise reaches GDP only as a tax on profits: current-price GDP per pound of 0.013 / 0.026 / 0.043 at q1 / q4 / q12, against the OBR's 0.35, fading. CPI is exogenous in the listing: l.314 is commented out and the retail markup absorbs the tax (l.284). The opt-in `run_reform(..., price_passthrough=True)` passes the tax into CPI through the listing's own cost-push weights (total indirect-tax weight 13.84 in `RPCOST`, l.270), with the markup held at baseline. VAT +1pp then lifts CPI by about 0.4% and gives 0.175 / 0.345 / 0.638 at q1 / q4 / q12 (0.26 averaged over year 1). That is close to the OBR at q4 but keeps building after, because nothing in the published equations makes consumption fade. It is a labelled judgement overlay, not the shipped listing ([#60](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/60)). [price_passthrough.md](price_passthrough.md) lists which price equations are held, shows the VAT run with and without the overlay, and sets out the proposal to keep the overlay opt-in, with the reasons on each side. |
| **Household tax multiplier** | The one channel with genuine behaviour (`dlog(CONS)` responds to real income). For a 1p basic-rate rise (£1,616m/quarter from 2025Q1, sustained; PolicyEngine's static costing, against HMRC's £1,725m used in [demo.md](demo.md), which gives the same ratios) current-price GDP per pound of revenue averages 0.16 in year 1, rising to 0.37 by quarter 12 under the default nominal-GDP pin, against the OBR's 0.3 impact multiplier decaying. That is the OBR's like-for-like basis. Real GDP per pound of current-price revenue, which this row used to quote, is 0.146 and 0.33: it mixes two bases and is smaller by PGDP/100 (1.08 in year 1, 1.14 at quarter 12; [#63](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/63)). `run_reform` reports both per quarter in `df.attrs["gdp_per_revenue_current_price"]` and `df.attrs["gdp_per_revenue_real"]`. Right order of magnitude, wrong profile. The household costing (`HHDI_ADDFACTOR`) moves household income only; receipts and borrowing do not move. Shock `TYEM` to move them too (±1,725 a quarter for HMRC's 1p). |
| **Corporation tax** | Requires `investment_closure=True`, which is a stop-gap. It freezes `MSGVA`, `PIF` and `PIRHH` to stop an explosive accelerator — unguarded, a **zero-shock** baseline runs business investment from £86bn in 2025Q1 to £522bn in 2027Q4 under the default nominal-GDP pin (£586bn under the published closure; £94bn to £8.0tn before the equation's bracket was corrected, [#59](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/59); re-measured 15 Nov 2026 on the March 2026 EFO; reproduce with `scripts/unguarded_ibusx.py`). The base-vs-shock *deviation* now converges to a steady state: the anchor add-factors are held in **log space** (the EViews convention for a `dlog` equation), so the published equation's own error-correction term pulls the deviation to `dlog(KSTAR) = −0.4·dlog(TAF)`. (Until 2026-08 the anchors were *level* add-factors, which amplified the deviation by 1.21–1.27× per quarter — `delta_IF` for +5pp hit £43.4bn by q25 with no steady state.) Measured now for a sustained +5pp rise: £0.24bn at q8, £0.38bn at q12, £0.68bn at q25, approaching a ~£0.96bn/q plateau; the error-correction root is slow (~0.958/quarter), so a 12-quarter figure is **~40% of the plateau** — `df.attrs["investment_closure_plateau_fraction"]` says where a run sits. The response size is set by `DB`, `DP`, `DV` (present values of capital allowances), now **estimated from statute and the OBR's published gilt assumption** rather than invented — see below. No OBR corporation-tax multiplier is quoted here: the multiplier table this repo encodes from EFO July 2015 Box 3.2 (`obr_macro/published_conventions.py`) has no corporation-tax row, so the response has no sourced benchmark. A `TCPRO` shock moves the cost of capital only: receipts and borrowing do not move (a static yield would be about £4.5bn a quarter for +5pp). |
| **Bank Rate** | Wired to consumption and household interest flows only (`test_bank_rate_response_is_consumption_only`); corporate interest and the cost of capital do not respond (`test_corporate_lending_rate_is_not_evaluated_under_the_default_closure`). Inflation and unemployment respond by exactly zero by default (unemployment moves only with the opt-in `employment_response`). A +1pp rise held for four quarters lowers GDP by 0.149% at the peak with `monetary_conditioning=True`, which moves the mortgage rate at the OBR's own l.623 pass-through and is the reading consistent with the listing, and by 0.057% in the default run, which holds the mortgage rate. Household interest received has no equation and moves in neither run, so neither is a bound on the full effect; the default stays the one-variable shock (decision of 18 Nov 2026, [monetary_conditioning.md](monetary_conditioning.md)). Both are below the Bank of England's −0.2% to −0.35% after about a year for the same experiment (*The transmission mechanism of monetary policy*, Quarterly Bulletin 1999 Q2). Under the default nominal-GDP pin the response is proportional and symmetric (peak −0.0145% for +25bp, −0.0574% for +100bp, +0.0590% for −100bp, measured on the March 2026 EFO). Under the published closure (`nominal_gdp_pin=False`) it is not, because a late PIF jump dominates small shocks ([#50](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/50)). Retail rates, gilts, sterling and house prices are OBR conditioning inputs with no equation; the opt-in `run_reform(..., monetary_conditioning=True)` moves the mortgage rate with Bank Rate at the OBR's own l.623 pass-through (0.9) and leaves the unsourced channels off. It is a labelled judgement overlay, not model dynamics; CPI and unemployment still do not respond, by design. See [monetary_conditioning.md](monetary_conditioning.md). |
| **Solver convergence** | Under the default closure (the nominal-GDP pin) every quarter converges to the tolerance. On the October 2026 EFO (16 Nov 2026) every baseline and shocked solve in the demo battery exits on tolerance in 12 of 12 quarters, and the anchored solve has no non-converged quarter to 2032Q1. Measured on 11 Nov 2026 (March 2026 EFO) over 2025Q1–2031Q1: 25 of 25 quarters exit on tolerance in the baseline and the shocked solve for government consumption, the household tax, Bank Rate and government investment, and in the published-conventions solve; a corporation-tax run under the investment closure also gives 25 of 25. `df.attrs["solver_exit_status"]` reports it per run. "Converged" covers the equations that evaluate: those with missing inputs are dropped and keep their data values. Under the published closure (`nominal_gdp_pin=False`) no quarter converges: Gauss-Seidel exits on the stall break in every quarter, zero-shock runs included, because the nominal-GDP / `PIF` pair above is undetermined and extra iterations let `PIF` wander ([#50](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/50), [#42](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/obr-macroeconomic-model/issues/42)). A delta under that closure is a difference of two non-converged solves. |
| **Debt interest** | Exogenous in the listing: central government debt interest `DICGOP` has no equation, so a reform that raises borrowing, or a higher gilt yield, leaves it on its data path. The opt-in `run_reform(..., debt_interest=True)` finances the extra borrowing with gilts at the EFO's 20-year yield and reprices the gilts the EFO plans to issue when the yield is shocked. Government consumption +£1.25bn a quarter then adds £8m / £61m / £230m of interest at q1 / q4 / q12 (8.1% on the cost over three years); gilt yield +1 point from 2026Q2 adds £1.1bn in the first year and £3.8bn in the second. The overlay adds no GDP effect: GDP is the same with it on or off (a gilt-yield shock lowers GDP either way, through the listing's l.649). Conventional gilts only; applied to the EFO's own issuance the rule is 5% to 13% above the EFO's interest on new gilts. A labelled judgement overlay, off by default. See [debt_interest.md](debt_interest.md). |
| **Uncertainty** | None. Every result is a point estimate. |

Instrument-level guards added on top of this: shocking a variable that is
missing or NaN now raises instead of silently returning zero effect, and a
`TCPRO` shock that would push the corporation-tax rate outside `[0, 1)` is
refused — above 1 the user-cost formula has a pole and the model previously
returned a finite, unflagged "a 105% corporation tax raises investment by
£3.2bn".

## :material-plus-box-multiple: Additivity of household costings { #additivity-of-household-costings }

Quarterly household-costing paths are near-linear for ordinary policy sizes,
but not mathematically additive: the OBR consumption equation contains log
differences and an error-correction term. Tests bound the departure from the
sum of isolated quarterly impulses to 0.05% for £100m–£400m quarterly shocks.
That is additivity across quarters for small shocks inside the window, not
size-linearity or sign symmetry: at £1.6bn a quarter, under the default
nominal-GDP pin, the rise-plus-cut residual is 0.2% of the peak and the
4x-versus-4×1x gap 0.3% ([nominal_gdp_pin.md](nominal_gdp_pin.md); 125% and 51% under the
published closure).
