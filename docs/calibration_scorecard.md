# Calibration scorecard

How well does the **raw** model (add-factor residuals OFF) reproduce the OBR's
published baseline? The anchored model matches by construction, so it says
nothing; the raw-vs-OBR gap is what is left. **That gap measures this emulator,
not the OBR.** In the OBR's own model GDP (`GDPM`) is an exogenous input — the
expenditure identity solves for inventories (`DINV`) — so the OBR's model cannot
"miss" GDP. The gap is this repo's closure swap (GDP made endogenous, `DINV`
held at its EFO path) plus the OBR inputs and calibration constants that are not
published and had to be pulled from the ONS or seeded. Generated by
`obr_macro/calibration_score.py` (raw model vs EFO, 2025Q1–2027Q4).
**EFO vintage: October 2026** (re-anchored from the March 2026 EFO on 16 Nov
2026; the October-2025 model code is unchanged).

**Raw solve is de-seeded.** The solver's data frame is pre-filled with the EFO
path, so a period whose Gauss–Seidel iteration exits on a stall-break would
otherwise sit near the EFO seed and flatter the raw score. The scorecard now
re-solves each period after reseeding every live computed variable from the
model's own previous-period value, so no computed variable can score well
merely by inheriting the EFO seed. (The raw pass in `baseline.py` likewise uses
a fresh solver rather than re-solving the anchored one.)

**Bands:** "within band" = good or fair under per-metric thresholds — rates
within **1.0pp**, net balances within **1.5% of GDP**, levels within **10%
MAPE**. It is not a single "within 10%" cut.

**Data vintage:** the unpublished-input series are current-vintage ONS data
merged into an October-2026 EFO baseline (October-2025 model code); where the
ONS has revised history the identities don't close exactly against the EFO,
and that slack lands in the add-factors of the anchored model (and, here, in
the raw-vs-EFO gap).

**The headline trap:** a `0.00%` error usually does **not** mean a good forecast
— it means the model left the variable at the OBR's published value because its
equation is dead or exogenous (*passthrough*). Only variables the model actually
**computes** measure calibration. And **Employment scores 0.00% as a trivial
identity** (`ETLFS = HWA/AVH`, both passthrough inputs), not as a behavioural win.

## Honest score
Default closure (nominal-GDP pin), October 2026 EFO, 2025Q1–2027Q4:
- **11 of 21** headline variables are actually computed; the other **10 are passthrough**.
- Of the 11 computed: 2 are good (the trivial employment identity and the
  trade balance), 6 are fair, 3 are poor, and none is off.
- **8 of 11 land within band (73%)**: Real GDP, Consumption, Business
  investment, Household income, Real household income and the current account
  (as % of GDP) are fair, plus the two good lines. The unemployment rate, RPI
  inflation and Company profits are poor.
- Under the published closure 7 of 11 are within band. Its business
  investment is poor and its Company profits are off; both measure solver
  drift (nominal GDP and the investment deflator `PIF` are one identity
  written twice, so `PIF` random-walks in the de-seeded solve and profits
  follow; see the OSHH-floor notes below and issue #42), not fit.
- None of this is an OBR forecast error. It measures this emulator's closure
  (GDP made endogenous) and the inputs the OBR does not publish.

### RPI: a metric fix, not a model fix
The model computes `RPI` as the year-on-year RPI **inflation rate**
(`RPI = PR/PR(-4)*100 - 100`), not the RPI price **index**. The scorecard used
to compare that rate against the EFO `RPI` *index* (~400) as a level, which is a
category error — it read a meaningless **99.58%** "off". Scored against the EFO
`RPIGR` inflation series as a rate (pp), the honest error was **2.03pp** ("poor") when the metric was fixed; it is
2.32pp on the October 2026 vintage (table above; 1.71pp on the March 2026
vintage).
The residual is real and traces to the RPI index itself: `PR` (≈ the exogenous
`I7` normaliser) freezes once its unpublished history runs out around 2026Q2, so
modelled inflation decays toward zero over the tail of the horizon. That decay is
bounded by the `I7`/`PR` history the OBR does not publish forward, not by the
equation — hence "poor", not a bug to chase.

### The OSHH floor under Company profits and the incomes block
`OSHH` (households' operating surplus — mostly imputed rental) is
`OSHH = 12874 + 0.85*IROO - DIPHHmf`, with `IROO = (PRENT*POP16)/1000`. Evaluated
from the databank inputs this yields ≈ **£38bn**, almost exactly **half** the
OBR databank's ≈ **£77bn**. The gap lives in the additive base constant `12874`
and the level/vintage of the `PRENT` rent index feeding `IROO` — neither of which
the OBR publishes. Because `HHDI` **adds** `OSHH` (so household income runs ~15%
low) and `FYCPR` **subtracts** it (so company profits are inflated), this single
unpublished-constant gap accounts for a large share of the residual error in
**three** scored variables at once (HHDI, RHHDI, FYCPR). Re-tuning `12874`/`0.85`
to hit the £77bn target would be fitting to the answer, not calibration, so it is
left as a documented floor: the anchored add-factor absorbs it, and the raw score
is gated against *regression*, not chased to zero (the gate is
`test_raw_calibration_scorecard_does_not_regress` in
`tests/test_model_invariants.py`, run in the hard-gating invariants CI job; the
`calibration-report` CI job remains report-only).

> **This floor no longer reaches the raw score (checked 2026-11-05, issue #42).**
> `FullOBRSolver._anchor_oshh_to_ons` (`obr_macro/full_solver.py`) pins `OSHH`
> to the published ONS series `CAEN` with a structural add-factor, and
> structural add-factors apply in every solve, raw included (`solve_period`).
> So the `12874`/`PRENT` gap described above is absorbed before it reaches
> `FYCPR`, and it cannot explain the 82.77%. A static check with no solve —
> the `OS` and `FYCPR` identities evaluated on the loaded EFO + ONS inputs over
> 2025Q1–2027Q4 — gives 3.6% MAPE if `OS` is taken from the data, and 23.9%
> if `OS` is built from its own identity (`GDPMPS − FYEMP − MI − BPAPS −
> TPRODPS − SDI`, mixing EFO and ONS vintages, with the ONS components held
> flat past the 2026Q1 snapshot edge). That is the identity-only floor; the
> rest of the 82.77% comes from solved inputs.
>
> **The run (6 Nov 2026, issue #42) names the input: the investment deflator.**
> `PIF` (l.337) and `GDPMPS` (l.688) are the same identity, so the pair is
> undetermined and Gauss-Seidel keeps whatever `PIF` it starts from. The
> plain raw solve (EFO seed left in place) gives FYCPR 12.17%; the de-seeded
> solve this scorecard reports reseeds each quarter from the last, `PIF`
> random-walks (106.7 → −4.6 → 317.2 against an EFO of about 105), and FYCPR,
> a residual of nominal GDP, follows: 82.77%. The FYCPR reference therefore
> measures drift, not fit; any input change can move it by tens of points
> (adding DEBTU, #43, moves it to 38.17%). Pinning nominal GDP with the OBR's
> own commented-out l.690, `GDPMPS = PGDP·GDPM/100`, gives 18.04%, below the
> static floor above. That closure is a decision for #42 and is not applied
> here; no number in the table above changes.

## Net balances are scored as % of GDP — in *both* scorecards
Current account and trade balance are scored as a share of GDP, not as a % of
their own (tiny) value: a difference of two ~£240bn gross flows is meaninglessly
amplified by a %-of-own-value metric (it read 27% / 68% that way). This is the
OBR's own convention. Crucially it is now applied **consistently in both this
scorecard and the forecast scorecard** (`forecast.py`) — previously it lived only
in the forecast scorecard, which is the kind of selective metric that flatters a
headline. Scored the same way everywhere, the trade balance is *fair*; the
current account was *off* (4.17% of GDP when this was written) once
de-seeded, driven by a net investment-income (`NIPD`) overshoot, itself
bounded by the overseas rate-of-return normalisers the OBR does not publish.
On the October 2026 vintage it is fair: 0.91% of GDP under the default
closure and 1.03% under the published closure (table above).

## Verdict
Raw (un-add-factored) calibration is **partial but honest**. Under the default
closure on the October 2026 EFO, Real GDP, consumption, business investment,
the incomes block and the current account sit within band (fair), the trade
balance is good, and the trivial employment identity is exact. The
unemployment rate, RPI inflation and Company profits are poor but no longer
diverging. The ~10 dead or exogenous channels (exports, imports, prices,
labour demand, wages) are held at the OBR value.

Note these figures are **much better than early versions of this doc** (which
recorded 318% / 932% / 375% divergences), because the incomes, investment and
financial-account blocks were revived and re-wired, and the RPI 99.58% was a
scoring-metric category error now corrected. They are also **de-seeded**: the raw
solve reseeds every live computed variable from the model's own previous-period
value before solving, so no variable scores well merely by inheriting the EFO
seed left in the data frame. What error remains is bounded by the constants and
deflator/normaliser histories the OBR does not publish (the OSHH base constant,
the `I7`/`PR` RPI-index history, the overseas rate-of-return normalisers behind
`NIPD`); the CI gates these against *regression* rather than failing the build on
a target the published inputs cannot reach. Concretely:
`test_raw_calibration_scorecard_does_not_regress`
(`tests/test_model_invariants.py`) recomputes the scorecard and fails the
invariants job if any computed variable worsens by more than 20% relative to
the pinned reference values. The table above is a documentation snapshot; the
reference values of record live in that test and are tightened as
improvements land.
